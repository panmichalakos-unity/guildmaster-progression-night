# Guildmaster: Progression Night
### *Football Manager for MMO raid guilds.*

**Genre:** Management sim · **Platform:** PC / Steam (Early Access path) · **Audience:** Sim-management fans + current and former MMO raiders

---

## The Pitch
You don't swing the sword — you run the guild. *Guildmaster: Progression Night* puts you in the officer's chair of a raiding guild inside a renowned, old-school fantasy MMO. Recruit and read people, gear them through a brutal slow-progression curve, set the strategy for raid night, and hold a 20-strong raid roster of real personalities together through wipes, loot drama, and clashing egos — season after season, as the world reshapes itself with every expansion. Every clear feels **earned**, because you earned it — by managing the people, not the pixels.

## The Fantasy
Millions of players have led or lived in an MMO guild. Nobody has built the definitive sim of *that job*: the spreadsheets, the politics, the 2 a.m. "why didn't I get the drop" argument, the slow grind of dragging dozens of humans to a single boss kill. That untapped fantasy is what this game owns.

## The Long Game — From Pulls to Expansions
The campaign nests like a Football Manager career, but the world **resets every year**:

- **The Pull** *(seconds–minutes)* — one attempt at a boss, resolved live on the combat log.
- **Raid Night** *(a session)* — a handful of pulls across one or more bosses.
- **The Week** — 1–3 raid nights plus the management days between them.
- **The Season / Tier** *(a few in-game months)* — clear and farm a raid tier; race rival guilds for server-firsts.
- **The Expansion** *(yearly)* — **the meta resets.** New zone, new raids and bosses, a fresh tier of loot, and a balance patch that rewrites the rules. New classes and specs arrive, the gear treadmill restarts, yesterday's optimal comp gets nerfed, and the roster churns as players quit, return, and get poached. Catch-up gear keeps it renewal, not grind.
- **The Career** — expansions stacked into a long arc: your guild's growing legend, or its slow collapse.

This is the engine of replayability — live-service-style longevity without live-service development cost.

## Stakes — The Fall & the Rebuild *(optional)*
You don't just manage a guild — you can lose it. Sustained failure (dead progression, collapsed morale, a broken loot economy) drains **officer and member confidence**, telegraphed like Football Manager's board-confidence panel so it's never a rug-pull. Cross the line and you're **ousted** — the sacking moment. But your *Player* endures: reputation, relationships, and skill carry forward into a from-scratch **rebuild** — a new guild, a ragtag roster, a damaged reputation to repair. Toggle the sack risk **on** for hardcore stakes, **off** for a pure builder's sandbox.

## Core Loop — The Raid Week
Within each week:

- **Plan** — set the week's goal (progress a new boss, farm gear, trial a recruit) and schedule your nights. Push to a 3rd night when desperate — at a real morale and burnout cost.
- **Develop (between nights)** — run heroic dungeons to gear up and test specs, hold 1:1s, farm and craft consumables, scout and trial recruits, and defuse drama as it fires.
- **Raid Night** — lead the pulls (detailed below).
- **Aftermath** — distribute loot, absorb the fallout, update standings, and learn who's happy, who's plotting, and who just got poached.

## Raid Night — The Match Engine
The signature moment, modeled on a live football match. The pull unfolds as a watchable combat-log feed, and **you lead as much or as little as you want**:

- **Set the strategy** — assign tanks, healing, interrupts, and soaks; choose a per-boss approach.
- **Dictate consumables per fight** — pick which flasks, elixirs, potions, and food the raid burns on each pull. Premium consumables cost gold and farmed mats, so spending them on a progression attempt is a genuine decision — and ties straight back to your between-night crafting and farming.
- **React between pulls** — after a wipe or a kill, swap players, rotate in the fire-resistance set, bench a fatiguing healer, or call the wipe early to save consumables and repair gold.
- **Lead as much or as little as you want** — an **involvement slider** runs from *full manual* → *assisted* → *instant result*. Even on auto you can **seize control at the moments that matter**: the game surfaces key decision points (phase changes, big mechanics, cooldown windows) and you can pause anytime to direct a raider — *move to the safe spot, pop the haste burst, swap the tank, call the wipe* — then hand it back. Directives still route through the raider executing them, so a shaky hand can fumble even the right call; roster quality always matters. A *key-moments* highlight mode keeps nights fast.

## The Raider — Avatar & Player
Every raider is two linked layers. **The Player is the human and persists; the Avatar is their character and can be re-rolled, re-geared, or swapped.** That single relationship turns "ask my star to re-roll for the new expansion meta" into a real decision, not a stat edit.

- **Avatar — the character.** Class & spec (→ role); the raid utility they bring (battle-rez, a raid-wide haste burst, interrupts, dispels); synergy tags for comp-building; gear power (item level); readiness caps — *not raid-ready until uncrittable*, plus a swappable per-boss resistance set; and **spec difficulty**, the lever that makes human skill matter.
- **Player — the human.** Three skill facets (**Execution / Awareness / Consistency**); durable **Role Proficiency** (plus a per-Avatar Familiarity that ramps on a spec and resets on re-roll); psychology that drives drama (ego, loyalty, temperament, coachability, influence); and **life situation as a trade-off, not a tier** — the parent who only raids late but is loyal and unpoachable; the student who's always online but flaky. Volatile **states** (morale, fatigue, form, and a **weekly availability roll**) sit on top — and a thin week can cancel a raid night outright, freezing progression.
- **Personality is layered, not labeled.** Continuous traits drive the math; authored **quirks** (*Mentor, Clutch, Tilt-Prone, Grudge-Holder, Flaky Genius*) add special-case behavior; and the on-screen **archetype** (*Backbone, Mercenary Ace, Project, Headache*) is read *from* that cluster — so the label always matches the behavior, and raiders drift between archetypes as their story unfolds.
- **Optional — Player-Guildmaster.** Take a raid slot yourself. Your Guildmaster has an Avatar and Player profile like anyone else and runs on the *same resolution system* — no separate action layer. Leading from the front lends a morale aura, but now your own gearing competes for loot and "the loot-hungry GM" becomes a temptation you have to manage. Manager-only is the default; skin-in-the-game is a choice.

## The Locker Room — Chemistry & Conflict
Drama runs deeper than loot and wipes. Every raider sits in a living social graph that quietly shapes performance:

- **History matters.** Players who've raided together before bring chemistry — better comms, covering each other's mistakes, a synergy bonus when grouped.
- **Rivalries bite.** Poach a bloc from a competing guild and they may drag their feuds in with them. A healer who hates the new tank "forgets" to top them — *no healz* — and underperforms when assigned to support someone they dislike.
- **Cliques and culture.** Friend groups, locker-room leaders, and the one toxic player who poisons morale. Lose the wrong veteran and a clique can fracture and walk.
- **You hold it together.** Pair people in dungeon runs to build bonds, split enemies across raid groups, mediate disputes — or cut your best DPS because the room is better without them.

## Design Pillars
- **Read the room, not the DPS meter.** Raiders are characters — skill, consistency, awareness, ego, loyalty, attendance, burnout — embedded in a web of relationships that drives emergent stories.
- **Progression that respects the grind.** TBC/Vanilla-era pacing: attunement gates, resistance and gear checks, multi-week boss kills, consumable prep. Loot is scarce, so every drop matters.
- **Loot is the drama engine.** Choose your system — DKP, Loot Council, /roll, Soft Reserve — each trading fairness for efficiency. Hand the best-in-slot drop to your most loyal raider or the night's MVP, and live with it.
- **Pressure is the antagonist.** Wipes drain morale; over-raiding burns people out. The push-now-vs-protect-the-roster squeeze is the central tension.
- **Legible cause and effect.** Good prep + smart assignments + healthy people → clean clears. Neglect any pillar and it shows. *"We cleared it because I handled it right."*
- **Play it your way.** Full manual to full auto, with the deep social and consumable systems surfaced clearly — not buried. Deep enough for the optimizers, readable enough that a sim-curious newcomer jumps in without a wiki.

## Market & Positioning
- **One-liner:** *Football Manager meets a classic MMO raid guild.*
- **Neighbors:** Football Manager, Out of the Park Baseball, Motorsport Manager (systems depth); Wrestling Empire / Total Extreme Wrestling (booking + drama); Classic WoW (proves the appetite for hard, old-school MMO nostalgia).
- **Who buys it:** Management-sim diehards, the millions of lapsed and current raiders who *lived* this, and optimization players who love planning over reflexes.

## Why It Works
- **Untapped, IP-free fantasy** with a large, identifiable audience and built-in nostalgia.
- **Emergent stories** — Football Manager's real product — come for free from people, loot, pressure, and a living social graph. Endlessly replayable and marketing-friendly.
- **Built-in renewal.** The yearly expansion resets the meta — new content, new builds, new drama — for live-service longevity without live-service cost.
- **Small-team feasible.** Systems- and text-driven like FM: high design depth, low art burden. Achievable scope for a lean team, with a clear Early Access → seasonal-content roadmap.
